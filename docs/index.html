Executive summary

The dates are bookable and the prices are real. I captured actual seven-night totals on 25 November 2026 for 15–22 February 2027, the preferred window. The same seven nights run from $556 at the Luxor to $5,828 at the Wynn. Every figure on the Hotels page is a rate that was genuinely quoted, with a link to the page that quoted it.

Las Vegas has no subway. The only rail is the privately run Las Vegas Monorail, seven stations between MGM Grand and SAHARA on the east side of the Strip. Everything else is bus. Your "one hour by subway or bus" requirement therefore had to be implemented as Monorail plus RTC bus, and that substitution is stated on every table that depends on it. Reading the requirement literally would return zero restaurants.

The most useful discovery is that Chinatown is reachable after all. Seven of the 24 MICHELIN restaurants — nearly a third of the list — sit on Spring Mountain Road in Chinatown, the densest concentration in the city. I initially assumed that road had no bus service and scored it as unreachable from every Strip hotel. That was wrong: RTC Route 203 runs the length of Spring Mountain Road and its published timetable carries a timed stop at Las Vegas Boulevard, 14 minutes from Decatur. Correcting that mistake raised every Strip hotel's count and, more importantly, changed which hotels win.

Wynn has the best access, for a reason nobody would guess. Wynn has no Monorail station, which sounds disqualifying. But Spring Mountain Road meets Las Vegas Boulevard at the north end of the Wynn property, so Route 203 — the only route to those seven Chinatown restaurants — boards a five-minute walk away. Wynn reaches 22 of 24, the highest in the study. Its problem is price: $5,828 for the week, against roughly $1,000 at Planet Hollywood.

Planet Hollywood and Flamingo are the two hotels I would actually book, and they tie on what matters. Both get 13 of 24 restaurants within 30 minutes — the best figure in the study — and both have the Horseshoe/Paris or Flamingo Monorail station a few minutes away. Planet Hollywood costs $1,026.17 fully refundable and puts six restaurants within walking distance, the most of any hotel here. Flamingo costs $625.65, also refundable, with a station literally at the property, but only four restaurants on foot and the weakest guest score in the shortlist at 7.8/10. Choose Planet Hollywood to eat out on foot; choose Flamingo to save about $400 and accept a slightly tired room.

One result needs a warning attached. Trump International reaches 21 of 24, second only to Wynn, for $1,883 — that looks like a steal and it is not what it appears. It reaches that many restaurants because it sits near Spring Mountain Road, which puts Route 203 to Chinatown within reach, but it gets zero restaurants within 30 minutes. Every single meal is a bus ride or a walk along a highway frontage. It is a fine hotel in the wrong place for this trip, and it is included to show that a high one-hour count can be misleading on its own.

Two facts force a decision before you book. The Cosmopolitan, which holds two MICHELIN restaurants, is completely unavailable for 15–22 February 2027 — it is only bookable 9–16 March at about $2,094. And Michael Mina at Bellagio, still on MICHELIN's 2026 list, is marked "Temporarily closed" by Bellagio itself, so it cannot be booked at all.

Why Wynn ranks first and MGM Grand does not. MGM Grand holds two starred restaurants in the building and a Monorail station inside the resort, which makes it the best hotel for a starred dinner. But it is the farthest south, so the seven Chinatown restaurants and the north Strip both cost it more than an hour. Wynn sits where Spring Mountain Road meets the Boulevard, so the Chinatown cluster is 34 minutes away. Different hotels win different questions — the table above answers only the one you asked.
