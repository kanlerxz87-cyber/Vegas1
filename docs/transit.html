Transit

Why "one hour by subway or bus" needed interpreting, the model behind the ranking, and the two errors it corrects

There is no subway in Las Vegas. Not a technicality — it changes the entire answer. The city has no metro, light rail or commuter rail. The only rail is the Las Vegas Monorail: a short privately operated line on the east side of the Strip, seven stations, MGM Grand to SAHARA. Everything else is bus. The requirement was therefore implemented as Monorail plus RTC bus, and that is stated wherever the result is used.

Correction: Chinatown is reachable. An earlier version of this page stated that Chinatown had no Deuce and no bus service and was therefore over an hour from every Strip hotel. That was wrong. Route 203 serves Spring Mountain Road directly and its official timetable proves it. Because Chinatown holds 7 of the 24 MICHELIN restaurants, the correction materially changed every hotel's score. RTC's own Route 203 timetable is on this page, with its timed stops listed, so you can check it yourself.

How the travel times were produced

Rather than invent minute values, every number on this site comes from the generator in tools/build_matrix.py. Exactly one travel time in the model is published; the rest are declared assumptions, printed below so you can see which is which and change them.

InputValueVerified or assumed
Walking speed3.0 mphAssumed
Monorail line speed15.0 mphAssumed
Deuce average speed8.0 mphAssumed
Wait per boarding8.0 minAssumed, roughly half the Monorail's published 4–8 min headway
Transfer penalty10.0 minAssumed
Route 203, Decatur to Las Vegas Blvd14.0 minPublished in the RTC timetable

Only the Route 203 Decatur-to-Las-Vegas-Blvd figure of 14 minutes is published. The speeds and penalties above are ordinary planning values, declared so the reader can see exactly which numbers are measured and which are assumed.

The two mistakes this page used to contain

First, every hotel tied. My first matrix graded all Strip hotels identically, so the ranking was useless. A model that cannot separate the Cosmopolitan from the Luxor is not measuring anything, so I rebuilt it from street addresses.

Second, and worse, I wrote off Chinatown. I assumed Spring Mountain Road had no bus service and marked its seven restaurants as over an hour from everywhere. Route 203 proves otherwise, and because those seven are 29% of the list, the error had changed which hotels won. Both mistakes are kept here rather than quietly deleted, because a study that hides its corrections is worth less than one that shows them.

What this means for a plan

Chinatown is a single bus ride from the far north Strip. Route 203 crosses Las Vegas Boulevard at the north end of the Wynn property and runs west along Spring Mountain Road. From Wynn it is about 34 minutes door to door, which puts seven restaurants inside your one-hour rule.

From the south Strip it is a two-leg trip. From MGM Grand or Luxor the route is Monorail or Deuce north, then Route 203 west, which lands at roughly 65–70 minutes — just outside the limit. If those seven restaurants matter, staying north or accepting a rideshare is the only way to have them comfortably.

The Arts District is on the Deuce corridor, so Esther's Kitchen and SoulBelly BBQ stay reachable from anywhere on the Boulevard. The Gold Coast and Anima by Edo are not.
