Hotels and MICHELIN dining cost — one table

Your brief asked for the hotel total and the 2-adult restaurant cost side by side, so here they are in one table.

Exactly what the dining column measures: the cost for two people to eat dinner once at each MICHELIN-listed restaurant that is within 60 minutes by bus of that hotel and publishes its price. That is a quantity that can be checked. It is deliberately not a 7-night dining budget — inventing one would mean guessing how often you eat out, and no source can tell me that.

So read it this way: if you plan to try two MICHELIN dinners, your dining spend is roughly this column divided by the number of priced venues, times two. Venues with no published price are excluded and listed separately rather than estimated.

How to use this. The top four hotels are within $143 of each other across seven nights, so a $20/night fee difference can reorder them completely. But the same table also shows the robust result: no plausible resort fee moves a $480 hotel below a $2,300 hotel. The expensive end of this list is expensive no matter what the fees are, and the cheap end is cheap. The middle is where the fees decide, and only a live booking total at checkout can settle it.
